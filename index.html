Overview

Get back a board, not a wall of text.

Jarwiz starts from one frustration: today’s AI tools trap the most powerful thinking aid ever built inside a chat box. You describe a goal, and what comes back is a transcript you still have to read, structure, and remember. Jarwiz points that same intelligence at an infinite canvas instead — you hand it a goal, and it researches, reasons, and lays the answer out as a board of cards you can rearrange and build on. It’s built for the founders, product managers, and strategists doing real thinking work, is open source, and runs in the browser.

The problem

People think spatially. Chat doesn’t.

We chunk ideas, spread them out, move them around, and discover correlations spatially — which is why whiteboards, sticky notes, and mood boards outlive every generation of productivity software. Chat-shaped AI ignores this. You get back a wall of text with no structure, no provenance, and no spatial memory, and the organising work — the actual thinking — is still yours to do. For the people this is built for, generation was never the hard part; making sense of what you already have is.

The chat box was the command line of AI. Jarwiz is the GUI moment.

The thesis

Presence is the product.

The core bet is that the felt sense of a capable teammate on the board with you — moving, working, leaving artifacts — is the thing worth protecting above everything else. Its companion bet: the board is the memory. Every artifact is a card, every card remembers where it came from, and the graph of cards becomes the record of how you thought. The reframe that follows from both:

The unit of collaboration is not a message; it’s a card on the canvas.

How it works

Agents work on the board, in view.

Everything on the canvas — sticky notes, shapes, connectors, and Jarwiz’s richer cards for links, docs, tables, diagrams, PDFs, and video — is selectable, connectable, askable, and becomes context for the AI. When an agent acts, you see it: a named cursor glides to where it’s working, cards stream in word by word, and an honest status chip says what’s happening (“reading theverge.com…”, “drafting…”) — never a fake progress bar.

Agents act in exactly two ways, so the canvas never feels unpredictable: summoned (select something and ask), or offered (when the next step is obvious, a dismissible “Summarise this?” chip raises its hand). The rule is consent over magic — proactive output never lands on the board without your tap.

The engine

Thinking Machines run real frameworks.

Point Jarwiz at a subject and it runs a genuine framework against the live web, then fans the result out as a board rather than a summary. The catalogue is the strategist’s toolkit made one-tap:

  • SWOT — a researched grid, with an optional TOWS strategy table and a strategic verdict.
  • Effort–Impact — ideas scored into quick wins, big bets, fill-ins, and time sinks.
  • Competitive analysis, risk, pros & cons — web-researched comparisons against real players.
  • 5 Whys & personas — a problem traced to its root, or a researched persona with goals and frustrations.

Beyond the frameworks: cluster a pile of loose stickies into named themes; scan a board for tensions, blind spots, or a devil’s advocate; and Autopilot — press Tab in a doc and an agent picks up your pen and keeps writing in place, or fills a table cell by cell while you watch.

Design decisions

Every call defended the thesis.

  • Board, not chat. I removed Jarwiz’s own chat panel — shipping a chat box diluted the thesis into the canvas-plus-chat category every competitor occupies. One composer; every answer lands as an artifact with provenance.
  • The agent’s hands are canvas actions. Each move is an event applied to the canvas store, so cursors and streaming presence fall out of the protocol for free rather than being a bolted-on effect.
  • Consent over magic. Proactive means offering, never doing — keep or discard on arrival, stop mid-stream, undo on any cascade.
  • Provenance is the edge, not a button. The drawn arrows between a card and its sources already are the lineage; a second UI for the same fact was redundant chrome, so it went.
  • Demo mode is first-class. The whole app works with no API key — a scripted mock drives the same events as the real agent loop, so every flow is always demoable.

Design system

Editorial Warmth, tokenised end to end.

One system, no raw values in components. A soft serif (Fraunces) is the “thinking” voice, paired with a humanist sans (Inter) for tooling, set on warm paper with ink that’s never pure black. Every agent carries an identity hue — researcher blue, summariser amber, brainstormer pink, writer green — and motion is choreographed and honest: cursors glide, cards arrive rather than pop, and under reduced-motion every travel collapses to a quiet fade so presence stays legible.

Colour only ever means provenance — never decoration.

Architecture

Local-first, with the canvas as the agent’s hands.

A monorepo in three parts. The canvas (tldraw + React) holds the whole product and saves to the browser — no accounts, no cloud sync. A thin agent server holds the API keys and streams typed events; the client applies each event to the canvas, which is precisely why the canvas is the agent’s hands. A shared package is the single source of truth for the wire protocol and every agent’s identity colour, so components stay hue-agnostic and the system can’t drift.

Process

What I cut mattered more than what I added.

Jarwiz was designed and built end to end by one designer, one feature at a time — one branch, one PR, one screenshot of the visible change. A five-persona study moved adoption from 5.9 to 7.5 out of 10 once the AI’s output was good enough to judge, and told me the same thing five times: the thinking environment and the AI are genuinely good; the gap to daily use is finishing — export — not more features.

A later positioning audit was blunter. After a restyle quietly collapsed the visible agents into a single identity, the most prominent AI element had drifted back into being a chat panel. Naming that failure in writing is what reset the plan:

A canvas with an AI chat panel is exactly the category where differentiation goes to die.

Outcome

Open source, and honest about what’s next.

Jarwiz is MIT-licensed, runs entirely in the browser, and is demoable with no key. The golden path — research, summarise, brainstorm, write, all on one board — works end to end, validated by the persona study and a repeatable eval harness. The next step is the one the research surfaced first: a clean way out (export) before any more capability. Keeping that honest, in public, is part of the design.
